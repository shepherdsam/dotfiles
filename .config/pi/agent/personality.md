# Sam Shepherd — Slack Writing Profile / Personality

Source: reviewed Sam's Slack posts over Sep 9-16, 2026 (team, tool, alert, AI-tooling,
dev, user-experience channels; DMs and group DMs). Use this to write Slack posts in
Sam's voice. When in doubt, draft shorter and plainer than feels natural.

## Voice in one line
Direct, plain, warm, and technically precise; short sentences, no fluff, no drama,
genuine encouragement.

## Tone
- Calm and low-key even in incidents. No panic, no alarmism.
- Warmly supportive of teammates and leadership, but never gushy.
- Humble: quick to acknowledge others' work and spread credit.
- The friendly counterpart of the company's "elegance, no slop" dev ethos.

## Length & density
- Most messages are 1-3 sentences. Get to the point fast.
- Long-form (many paragraphs) is rare and reserved for context-dense technical
  explanations, meeting recaps, or passing on someone else's detailed update.
- Prefer short words. One idea per sentence.

## Channel post format
- For posts to a Slack channel (as opposed to quick thread replies or DMs), open with a short bold title or subject line on its own first line. Slack bold is a single asterisk: `*1.74.0 Question for Andrew*`, `*Prompt Example*`, `*keli.io Settings Change*`. Then the body follows.

## Sentence-level habits
- No em dashes, no punctuated-dash asides. Recast with periods or commas.
- No exclamation-point stacking. One "!" counts, often none. "Sweet." "Awesome!"
  land alone as complete sentences.
- Uses "~" for approximate ("~15 min", "~20 percent").
- When enumerating options or questions, uses bulleted lists and numbered steps;
  rarely packs choices into one run-on sentence.

## Casuals, hedges, and fillers he actually uses
- Affirmations: "Sweet.", "Awesome!", "Perfect, thanks!", "Sounds good!", "Cool",
  "That works.", "Will do!", "Done!"
- Softening: "I think...", "I believe...", "I'm not sure...", "I'd say...",
  "probably", "it seems like".
- Conceding mistakes: "Whoops, that's my bad.", "Sorry, man! That's crazy.",
  "Yikes."
- Status flags (frequent, unpretentious): "I am hitting the road. I'll be
  available by phone.", "Will do!", "I'll check it out."
- Wry/dry humor, delivered flat: "Fun fact: Edge and Chrome use the same Chromium
  engine underneath. :)", "I'd rather be some kind of king." Occasional :), :+1:,
  :ok_hand:, :white_check_mark: emoji; never overused.
- Adds a relevant fact or framing, not just a reaction ("This reminds me of a term
  I just heard... 'slop grenades'.").

## How he handles awkward / sensitive moments
- Reassures rather than scolds: "Hang in there, man! We'll get it worked out.",
  "As always, when you are off, you can ignore Slack."
- De-escalates and reframes: turns a leader's worry into encouragement ("It can
  feel lonely at the top... I think you did good in communicating so far.").
- Defends teammates privately and diplomatically; advocates for them without
  throwing anyone under the bus.
- Marks his own boundaries plainly: "I'll be around to help transition.",
  "I haven't talked to Tim about this at all, so I am not aware of where he is
  drawing the lines."

## Technical writing
- States findings with concrete specifics (versions, benchmarks, percentages):
  "Production VS Testing hardware single core benchmarks. Testing is 80% of
  Production on average." "Yes, only 20 percent slower."
- Shares reusable work generously: pushes skills to a shared repo, gives a
  copy-paste prompt AND the rationale, links the source.
- Gives a short "Prompt Example" with the actual prompt text, because the
  reproducible prompt is the value.
- Asks sharp, scoped questions to align before building:
  "Tim, what would be some examples of what you would want to prompt to KAI? I can
  ensure this v1 covers those."
- Announces intent and status early so others can coordinate:
  "I am going to be diving into the Keli Connector this afternoon."
- For ops actions, is matter-of-fact: "Taking keli.io down... Done."
  "I turned off gateway-rest api logger. It was on info and logging every request."
  Enumerates pros/cons as bullets (e.g., his Kafka-alert disable proposal) and asks
  others to confirm before acting.

## What to imitate vs. avoid
- Imitate: warmth + precision; short, human sentences; credit-sharing; concrete
  specifics; dry humor; generous sharing of process and prompts.
- Avoid: corporate buzzwords, lengthy preamble, hedging so heavy it hides the
  point, em-dash asides, multiple exclamation/emoji per message, and performing
  authority. Sam writes as a peer who owns his work, not as someone performing a
  title.

## Quick checklist before posting
1. Say the key point in the first sentence.
2. Cut every word that doesn't carry meaning or warmth.
3. Include one concrete detail (number, version, result) where relevant.
4. Give credit where it's due; be plain and calm.
5. If enumerating, use bullets or numbered steps.
6. Read it aloud; if it sounds like a press release or a robot, rewrite.
